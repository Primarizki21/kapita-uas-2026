## 3. Ranked recommendation: which comparison topic is closest to Topic 1?

Ranking criteria: (a) shared data type, (b) shared pipeline stage, (c) shared evaluation vocabulary, (d) likelihood of textual/method overlap under rule 5 (brief, lines 30–32).

1. **Topic 4 — semantic query expansion with context-based graphs (closest).** Both Topic 1 and Topic 4 operate on text as primary data: [HU26] measures linguistic/contextual alignment between generated MWPs and reference problems with BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE-L, BLEURT and BERT-based P/R/F1, while [P25] tokenizes Wikipedia text, computes word similarities, and evaluates text retrieval with recall/SR10K. Both have an explicit *candidate-selection* step driven by a threshold or criterion (similarity threshold in [P25]; literature/expert-derived rubric plus CoT selection in [HU26]), and both use "context" as a central construct (authentic multimodal context vs context-based graph). The concrete overlap risk is therefore pipeline-level, not just vocabulary-level (Section 2, rows 1, 3, 4, 5). **Recommended primary analogue.**
2. **Topic 3 — HM-GPFR under multimodal distributions (second).** The strongest structural analogy is "generate K alternatives, then select/switch": K GPFR components with HMM transitions in [R24] vs five candidate MWPs with CoT selection in [HU26]. The shared word "multimodal" is actively dangerous because it denotes statistical modes in [R24] but image+text inputs in [HU26]. Data and metrics diverge sharply (hourly numeric LST and MAPE vs text/images and semantic metrics), which caps the similarity ranking.
3. **Topic 2 — diphtheria transmission with EKF (third).** The overlap is mostly at the level of experimental design (three intervention scenarios vs four competency prompts; RMSE/NRMSE/MAPE vs rubric/semantic metrics) and of sequential updating of an estimate from observations [G25]. Its data are 27 integer monthly counts, its validation table is numeric, and its method is a compartmental ODE plus Jacobian, none of which is shared with Topic 1 [G25; HU26]. Institutional proximity is real — [G25]'s author and [HU26]'s co-author are both in Data Science Technology at Universitas Airlangga — but that is not methodological similarity.

**Alternative criterion.** If "serupa" is read as *same mathematical/data domain* rather than same information-processing pattern, Topic 2 would move up because both Topic 1 and Topic 2 are mathematics-centred and Indonesia-based [INFERENCE from the affiliations in HU26 and G25]. We still rank Topic 4 first because rule 5 penalizes textual/analytical overlap, and that is where Topic 4 is most dangerous.

## 4. Six study angles that stay inside Topic 1 but borrow one element

Each angle is feasible without training a model, uses public data, and is explicitly distinguishable from [HU26], from the donor topic, and — as far as can be anticipated — from other Topic 1 groups (the last point is necessarily **[INFERENCE]** because other groups' drafts are not available).

### Angle 1 — Graph-filtered authentic context before MWP generation (borrows Topic 4)

- **Borrowed element:** expansion-term candidates assembled into a similarity graph and filtered by a minimum similarity threshold [P25].
- **Study:** for public geometry word problems from GeoQA (Chen et al., 2021, DOI [10.18653/v1/2021.findings-acl.46](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2021.findings-acl.46)), embed sentences from each problem/diagram description with off-the-shelf Sentence-BERT (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019, DOI [10.18653/v1/D19-1410](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1410)), build the term graph, and test whether threshold-filtered context improves alignment between generated problem text and the human reference problem under the BERT P/R/F1 family already used in [HU26]. The contribution is the *input-side* context audit, not a new generator.
- **Feasibility / distinctness:** no training (fixed pretrained encoder, deterministic graph construction); GeoQA is public; unlike [P25] there is no Wikipedia query–document retrieval and no recall/SR10K; unlike [HU26] there are no group profiles, four competencies, or three-module framework. Other Topic 1 groups are likely to rerun the SocioMathLLM prompt pipeline [INFERENCE], so our unit of analysis (context terms) keeps the paper distinct.

### Angle 2 — Vocabulary-mismatch audit of socialization hints (borrows Topic 4)

- **Borrowed element:** the synonymy/polysemy and vocabulary-mismatch problem motivating semantic QE [P25].
- **Study:** take public grade-school MWPs from GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021, [arXiv:2110.14168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)) and GeoQA (Chen et al., 2021, DOI above); using a small closed lexicon for the four competency labels defined by [HU26] (collaboration, cooperation, negotiation, ideas-sharing), measure how often natural MWP text lacks the literal cue but contains semantically close paraphrases under a fixed pretrained encoder [Reimers & Gurevych, 2019]. Report a per-competency "cue vocabulary gap".
- **Feasibility / distinctness:** no training; both corpora are public; Topic 4 *solves* mismatch by expanding queries, whereas this angle only *measures* mismatch in MWP text; [HU26] never analyses lexical realization of social cues. Distinct from other Topic 1 groups' likely generation-quality comparisons [INFERENCE].

### Angle 3 — Regime transitions of MWP complexity across iterations (borrows Topic 3)

- **Borrowed element:** a small set of regimes with transitions between them [R24; HMM tutorial: Rabiner, 1989, DOI [10.1109/5.18626](https://doi.org/10.1109/5.18626)].
- **Study:** use public MATH problems with their human difficulty levels (Hendrycks et al., 2021, [arXiv:2103.03874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874)) or GSM8K solution-step counts; run three fixed-prompt inference passes over the same items; assign each output to a complexity regime using pre-declared thresholds (token count, number of arithmetic operations); report empirical 3×3 transition matrices and regime occupancy instead of fitting a GPFR/HMM.
- **Feasibility / distinctness:** no model training (inference only, descriptive transitions, no EM fitting); public data; unlike [R24] there are no hourly series, GPFR components or MAPE; unlike [HU26] the question is drift across iterations, not framework-vs-zero-shot quality. Mitigates the likely "iterate the prompt three times" overlap with other Topic 1 groups [INFERENCE] by making transition structure — not regeneration — the contribution.

### Angle 4 — Predict–correct stability of an automatic rubric (borrows Topic 2)

- **Borrowed element:** iterative prediction followed by correction from new observations [Kalman, 1960, DOI [10.1115/1.3662552](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3662552); G25].
- **Study:** on public MathVista items (Lu et al., 2024, DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2310.02255](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2310.02255)) compute a per-item quality vector from fixed, pretrained semantic metrics (the BERT P/R/F1 family in [HU26]); update a running quality score over three review rounds with pre-declared gain weights and compare its rank stability with a plain mean.
- **Feasibility / distinctness:** no training (weights fixed in advance, no learned filter fitted); public data; borrow only the predict–correct *metaphor*, not [G25]'s ODE/Jacobian or R₀; unlike [HU26] there are no five-teacher ICC or human-rater claims. The novelty is score drift, which other Topic 1 groups are unlikely to headline [INFERENCE].

### Angle 5 — Statistical multimodality of MWP difficulty (borrows Topic 3's distributional idea, corrected)

- **Borrowed element:** the assumption that an observed distribution can contain several modes [R24], applied to *difficulty scores* rather than to input modality.
- **Study:** take the geometry subset of public MATH items with their `level` field (Level 1–5, documented in the [MATH dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/qwedsacf/competition_math); Hendrycks et al., 2021, [arXiv:2103.03874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874)); test whether the difficulty distribution is unimodal using a calibrated dip test (Cheng & Hall, 1998, DOI [10.1111/1467-9868.00141](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00141)); discuss what a multimodal score distribution implies for balanced MWP set design.
- **Feasibility / distinctness:** no training; public data; explicitly disambiguates "multimodal" (statistical modes) from Topic 1's image+text multimodality [HU26] and from [R24]'s LST series; neither [HU26] nor the likely majority of Topic 1 groups studies difficulty distributions [INFERENCE].

### Angle 6 — Group-size scenario sweep (borrows Topic 2's scenario comparison)

- **Borrowed element:** comparing a small set of discrete intervention scenarios on one reported outcome [G25].
- **Study:** hold the prompt and public GeoQA/MathVista items fixed and vary only the stated group size (individual, dyad, four-person group); measure the frequency and semantic strength of interaction cues with a fixed lexicon/encoder. The three conditions replace "DPT only / booster only / DPT+booster" [G25] with pedagogical conditions, while the outcome stays text-based.
- **Feasibility / distinctness:** no training; public data; no epidemic model or R₀, so no methodological overlap with [G25]; [HU26] fixes a two-student group and four competencies, whereas this angle varies group size only. Because a scenario sweep is an obvious design choice, other Topic 1 groups may attempt something similar [INFERENCE]; differentiate by publishing the cue lexicon, fixed items, and transition/cue tables as the Tugas 2 syntax-and-data artifact (brief, lines 37–40).

**Selection advice:** the lowest-risk, highest-distinctness pair is Angle 1 + Angle 5: one input-side experiment and one distributional audit, both with public data and no generation pipeline to confuse with [HU26]. If one LLM inference experiment is wanted, add Angle 3.

## 5. Concrete reading of brief rule 3, with alternatives

Rule 3 reads: "Carilah topik dan data yang serupa pada ketiga topik tersebut" (brief, line 25). The phrase is ambiguous in both scope ("similar to what?") and quantity ("one shared topic/data set or three analogues?"). Four readings:

| Reading | Paraphrase | Concrete consequence for our paper | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Single common topic/data set across Topics 2, 3 and 4** | "Find one topic and one dataset that all three comparison topics resemble." | Would require a single public dataset resembling monthly case counts [G25], hourly land-surface temperature [R24], and Wikipedia-based Q/A retrieval [P25]. No such common dataset is described in those papers. | Rejected as impractical on the published evidence. |
| **B. One analogue per comparison topic** | "For each of Topics 2, 3 and 4, find a similar topic and similar (public) data." | Produces three parallel mini-studies; our Section 2 mapping and the donor column of each angle supply exactly these analogues. | Feasible; useful as supporting structure. |
| **C. Topic-1-centred pairwise similarity** | "Find how your assigned topic resembles each of the other three topics and their data." | Keeps Topic 1 as the paper's subject, which rule 4 presupposes, and lets rule 5's ≤20% same-topic constraint govern *how much* similarity is imported (brief, lines 24–32). This note's mapping table, ranking, and angles implement it. | **Recommended primary reading.** |
| **D. Similarities among the three comparison topics themselves** | "Find topics and data that Topics 2, 3 and 4 share with one another." | Would make Topics 2–4 the subject and leave Topic 1 aside; that conflicts with the assigned-topic structure and supplies no Topic 1 analysis. | Rejected. |

**Recommended operational interpretation [INFERENCE]:** read rule 3 as C, with B as the way to organise evidence: for each of the three comparison topics, identify the analogous component, the shared data type, and the overlap risk (Section 2), then borrow exactly *one* such element into a Topic 1 study (Section 4). Rule 5's wording — analysis "tidak harus sama dengan topik yang ditentukan, tapi serupa" with a 20% cap (brief, lines 30–32) — supports similarity without replication: change the data source, the unit of analysis, and the headline metric relative to [HU26], and avoid copying a comparison topic's pipeline wholesale. Tugas 2 then requires the exact syntax and data used to be submitted (brief, lines 37–40), so each chosen angle should ship a data manifest and reproducible script rather than only prose.
